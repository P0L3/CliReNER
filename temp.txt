## The Callaghan framing needs to change — this isn't optional polish

I pulled the actual Callaghan et al. (2021) abstract to double-check your read: *"we use the language model BERT to **identify and classify** studies on observed climate impacts, producing a comprehensive machine-learning-assisted evidence map."* That's document-level classification of ~100k papers into a coarse label set — no span-level entity extraction. Your comment is exactly right, and the current draft's "applied text classification **and extraction**" quietly claims something the paper doesn't do.

This matters more here than it would for a random citation, because R1.Rm4's whole complaint was that the "scientific community increasingly relies on NLP/NER" claim was *unsubstantiated* — if the fix for that introduces a citation that overstates what its source actually does, and a reviewer who knows this fairly well-known Nature Climate Change paper notices, you've replaced one credibility problem with a worse one. R3 in particular reads closely.

The good news: the honest version of this claim is actually **more persuasive**, not less. Reframe it as: even coarse, manually-curated document classification has already proven valuable enough to publish in Nature Climate Change — and the bottleneck is that it's manual/coarse. That's a stronger motivation for fine-grained NER than pretending NER already happened there. Suggested replacement:

> Machine-learning-assisted evidence synthesis has already demonstrated the value of automated literature analysis directly in the climate domain: \citet{Callaghan2021evidence} used a fine-tuned language model to classify roughly 100,000 climate-impact studies, exposing systematic gaps in the geographic attribution of climate evidence, while a comparable classification pipeline over climate-policy literature \citep{callaghan2025climatepolicy} revealed sectoral and regional disparities between scientific attention and enacted policy. These pipelines operate at the document level, however, relying on manually curated, coarse label sets; fine-grained NER would extend this pattern to the entity level, automatically supplying the typed building blocks — drivers, affected systems, locations, measurements, and policy responses — that such evidence-mapping pipelines currently obtain only through manual screening.

This keeps your "+1 concrete climate example" requirement satisfied (R1.Rm4) and your Table 1/entity-saturation forward-pointer still slots in right after it.
